Test staging, and get it in writing first

Run this against a staging clone, not live tenants — the every-minute cron sweeps send real email and the Paystack keys move real money. Before any test, hold written authorization naming the scope, the window, and the environment. Keep third-party infra (8x8 JaaS media servers, Paystack’s own systems, the hosting provider’s hardware) out of scope; you test your integration with them, never their platforms.

Part A

Security

The headline risk in this system is cross-tenant data leakage: one institute owner, staff member, or student reaching another institute’s records. Everything below is weighted toward that, plus the payment webhook, the three bearer-token flows, and the hand-signed JaaS tokens.

01

Recon & information gathering

Map the real attack surface before touching it. This app has three portals (/lms/app student, /lms/staff, /lms/admin owner), a Botble super-admin panel, per-institute storefronts at /i/{slug}, and a Laravel API the Next.js frontend calls.

Surface mapping

Part B

Scaling & Performance

The constraint that shapes everything: shared hosting, a single MySQL, and synchronous mail sent by an every-minute cron with no queue worker. Load testing here is less about a huge concurrent-user number and more about which of those three chokes first — and what happens when a marketing push, a renewal season, or a scheduled CEO Forum lands all at once.

08

Load testing strategy

Four test shapes, each mapped to a real Jorsas scenario. Always run against a staging clone with production-shaped data volume (seed realistic tenant/student/enrolment counts — an empty DB hides every N+1).

Baseline
Normal weekday

Establish the reference: dashboard loads, materials/timetable fetches, chat polling (5s student, 15s staff), a trickle of logins.

Steady, ~expected DAU concurrency, 15–30 min
Stress
Enrolment surge

Ramp signups + pay-first checkouts + course invites until error rate climbs or latency breaks threshold. Find the ceiling.

Ramp to breakpoint, hold, observe failure mode
Soak
Renewal season

Sustained moderate load for hours. Surfaces connection leaks, memory growth, and cron sweeps falling behind their backlog.

60–70% of breakpoint, 2–8 hours
Spike
Forum + announcement

All institute owners join a CEO Forum at once (token mint burst) while a platform announcement fans out email to every tenant.

Sudden step to peak, recover, re-measure
IsolateThe 8x8 JaaS media load is out of scope (it’s their infrastructure). What you load test is your server’s token minting under a join burst — the RS256 signing and the DB reads around it, not the video conference itself.

Per-finding record

Log every finding — security or performance — with the same fields so they rank on one combined remediation list:

FieldWhat to record
ID & titleStable ref (e.g. SEC-003 / PERF-002) and a one-line summary
Type & severitySecurity severity or performance class from the scales above
ComponentEndpoint, portal, controller, or scenario affected
EvidenceRequest/response (redacted), metric chart, query log, or repro steps — no live PII
ImpactWho/what is affected and the business consequence (tenants breached, users blocked at peak)

StatusOpen → In progress → Fixed → Retested & verified
PriorityRank the two scales on one list by real-world consequence, not by discipline. A CRITICAL tenant-isolation break and a P1 connection-exhaustion cliff are peers — both can stop the platform — and should be triaged together, above every HIGH/P2 regardless of which half of the audit found them.
